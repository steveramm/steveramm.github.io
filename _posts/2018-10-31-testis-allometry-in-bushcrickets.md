![IMG\_1391]({{ '/assets/images/testis-allometry-in-bushcrickets/img_1391.jpg' | relative_url }})In putting together the last two posts I realised I'd neglected to post about another paper that came out in 2018, so to put that right...

Earlier this year it was a great pleasure to see published [this study](http://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/bly018) by Bielefeld Master students Mark Koschmieder and Martin Müller (which I co-supervised together with [Klaus Reinhold](http://www.uni-bielefeld.de/\(en\)/biologie/Evolutionsbiologie/mitarbeiter/reinhold.html)) investigating the evolution of testis allometry in bushcrickets. We focussed on comparing the allometric scaling of testis size in two subspecies of _Poecilimon veluchianus,_ to address why their recent divergence in body size (see e.g. [here](http://doi.org/10.1111/een.12061)) has not been accompanied by similar changes in the relative size of the testes. We found that both subspecies exhibit positive testis allometry, as expected if sperm production is under strong sexual selection, but have diverged markedly in the allometric intercept. The finding adds to evidence from other taxa for the greater evolvability of allometric intercepts.

This was a further publication to come out of our annual field course in Greece on the behavioural ecology of bushcrickets for students on the Master programme [Behaviour: From Neural Mechanisms to Evolution](https://www.uni-bielefeld.de/biologie/behaviour/). The whole course is packed in to just six weeks, so it's a testament to Mark and Martin's hard work that they could first of all generate a high quality dataset in that time, and then put in the extra work after the course to turn their data into a publishable manuscript.

The photo is the view towards Mt. Velouchi from our field site, Central Greece.

![sf-net1]({{ '/assets/images/macrostomum-seminal-fluid/sf-net1.jpg' | relative_url }})Delighted to report that both [Michael](http://www.uni-bielefeld.de/\(en\)/biologie/Evolutionsbiologie/mitarbeiter/weber.html) and [Bahar](http://www.uni-bielefeld.de/\(en\)/biologie/Evolutionsbiologie/mitarbeiter/patlar.html) have now published the first papers from their PhD projects on the functional and evolutionary genetics of seminal fluid in _Macrostomum_. Michael’s [paper](http://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-018-1187-0) sets out to identify candidate transcripts that encode seminal fluid proteins in _M. lignano_, by performing in situ hybridization experiments to narrow down a long list of potential seminal fluid genes based on previous RNA-Seq data. The prostate-limited transcripts identified are the ones we’ve subsequently been focusing on to try to figure out their functional and fitness relevance. Bahar’s [paper](https://rdcu.be/9YIc) is a further step in that direction, using a panel of inbred lines and high-throughput gene expression analyses to partition variance in seminal fluid expression into genetic and environmental components. We see modest social environment effects on seminal fluid expression, but clear evidence for strong genetic effects and some hints for genotype x environment interactions. Interestingly, we find that multivariate variation in seminal fluid expression can be captured by relatively few axes that likely describe changes in the amount and composition of seminal fluid. Both papers serve to establish _M. lignano_ as a good hermaphroditic model system for studying seminal fluid-mediated fitness effects, and lay the groundwork for the functional studies we’ve also now completed (watch this space!).

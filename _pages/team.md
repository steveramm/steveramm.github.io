<div class="team-member">
<h2 class="team-member-name">Quentin Corbel</h2>
<p><img src="/images/team/quentin.jpg" alt="Quentin Corbel" class="team-photo"> Postdoc. Quentin is working on the ANR project FRIDA, in collaboration with Sylvain Glémin at Ecobio and collaborators in Montpellier, Perpignan and Lyon. Together we are interested in using two selfing animal species to decipher the relative contributions of genetic and epigenetic effects to a fundamental phenomenon in evolutionary biology, inbreeding depression. Quentin joins us following a recent postdoc at CNRS Moulis and a PhD at the University of Valencia on adaptive reproductive plasticity and life-history trade-offs in <em>Drosophila</em>.</p>
<div style="clear: both;"></div>
</div>
<div class="team-member">
<h2 class="team-member-name">Elise Jeanne</h2>
<p><img src="/images/team/elise.jpg" alt="Elise Jeanne" class="team-photo"> PhD student. As part of the project “Fertility in a Changing World”, Elise is investigating plastic and evolutionary responses of flatworms to altered thermal and salinity environments. Her project aims to clarify sex differences in environmentally-imposed fertility limits and test the interaction between natural and sexual selection in driving adaptation to climate change.</p>
<div style="clear: both;"></div>
</div>
<div class="team-member">
<h2 class="team-member-name">Sarthak Grover</h2>
<p><img src="/images/team/sarthak.jpg" alt="Sarthak Grover" class="team-photo"> PhD student. In his project “Selfing Under Stress”, Sarthak is investigating potential links between environmental variation, inbreeding depression and mating system expression. Sarthak initially joined the lab in Bielefeld in 2022 while he was a BS/MS student at IISER Mohali, completing his Master thesis research on mating system expression and sex allocation in the flatworm <em>Macrostomum hystricinum</em>.</p>
<div style="clear: both;"></div>

<h2>Alumni</h2>
</header>
<div class="page-content">
<p><strong>Kélian Salvadego</strong> – M2 intern (2026). A student on the EFCE Master programme in Rennes, Kélian worked with Sarthak to quantify the relationship between environmental stress and inbreeding depression, and then stayed on to help us in the lab maintaining stocks and experimental cultures over the summer.</p>
<p><strong>Léo Jamon</strong> – M1 intern (2026). A student on the EFCE Master programme in Rennes, Léo worked with Elise to study the experimental evolution of thermal adaptation.</p>
<p><strong>Clara Crozatier</strong> – M1 intern (2026). A student on the BEE Master programme at Aix Marseille Université, Clara joined the lab to work with Elise on the contributions of natural and sexual selection to adaptation under novel thermal and salinity regimes.</p>
<p><strong>Shaman Dor-Vincent</strong> – M1 intern (2026). A student on the IMABEE Master programme in Rennes, Shaman investigated the molecular basis of sex allocation plasticity in <em>Macrostomum lignano</em>.</p>
<p><strong>Sébastien Bruzzo</strong> – M2 intern (2025). Sébastien performed experiments to understand the mechanistic basis of heat and salinity-induced insults to fertility. His project tested how chronic and acute stressors (singly or in combination) affect flatworm fertility; whether those have differential impacts on the male or female sex function; and examine some of the key traits that are likely to mediate those responses, such as sperm morphology and mating behaviour. Currently being written up!</p>
<p><strong>Lucas Bernard</strong> – M1 intern (2025). A student on the EFCE Master programme in Rennes, Lucas worked with Sébastien and Elise on experiments to understand the mechanistic basis of heat and salinity-induced insults to fertility.</p>
<p><strong>Eva Esteve</strong> – M1 intern (2025). Joining us from the Ecophysiology, Ecology and Ethology Master at Uni Strasbourg, Eva worked on climate change and fertility, with a particular focus on sperm-mediated impacts of heat stress.</p>
<p><strong>Emma Lavaut</strong> – (2024-2025) as an LRU lecturer-researcher at ECOBIO, Emma was interested in how environmental stressors affect the operation of sexual selection, and how this can be quantified using Bateman gradients and other metrics.</p>
<p><strong>Elena Breitenberger</strong> – M2 intern (2024). Elena used meta-analysis (in collaboration with Liam Dougherty in Liverpool) to investigate multi-stressor impacts on animal fertility, with an eventual focus on salinity that found some really strong and important effects – currently being written up for publication!</p>
